# Your Intelligence Product

An intelligence agent that produces no actionable output is a data collector, not an intelligence capability. The final step in any agent design is defining the intelligence product it delivers and who consumes it.

## The Intelligence Product Definition

An intelligence product has three components:

1. **Form** — What does the output look like? (Brief, dashboard, email digest, structured report)
2. **Content** — What specific intelligence does it contain? (Competitor wins, award trends, leadership changes, market shift indicators)
3. **Consumer** — Who receives it, at what frequency, and what decision does it support?

## Strong vs. Weak Product Definitions

**Weak:** "A report on competitors"

**Strong:** "A weekly 1-page digest delivered every Monday to the BD Director, listing: (1) any new awards ≥ $5M by the top 5 competitors in our target domain, (2) any new solicitations in that domain closing within 60 days, (3) one trend observation from the past week. Purpose: support Go/No-Go pipeline decisions."

The strong version tells you exactly what to build, who owns it, and what decision it enables.

## Reflection Prompt

Answer three questions:

1. What specific intelligence product would this agent produce? (Describe form, content, and frequency in 2–3 sentences.)

2. Who is the primary consumer, and what decision does this product inform?

3. What is the one signal that, if the agent surfaces it, would immediately change a business or operational decision?
