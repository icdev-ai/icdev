# Your 90-Day Workforce Plan

A workforce strategy that has no names, no dates, and no budget is a vision statement. A 90-day plan is a commitment.

## Why 90 Days

Ninety days is long enough to see results but short enough to maintain urgency. It also aligns with typical quarterly planning cycles, which means it can be funded and reviewed in the same cycle it is proposed.

## What a Strong 90-Day Plan Includes

- **3 to 5 named individuals** — Not role categories. Specific people who are ready, willing, and strategically positioned to benefit from early AI upskilling.
- **A clear "why" for each** — What makes this person a high-leverage investment? Mission knowledge? Influence over others? Leadership position? Technical aptitude?
- **A specific training path** — Not "send them to training." Name the program, the format, and the expected outcome.
- **One team-level outcome** — What will this team be able to do in 90 days that they cannot do today?

## Why These 3–5 People First

Early AI adopters inside an organization become its most effective change agents. They build credibility through demonstrated results, not theory. Invest in the people who will use new skills immediately and visibly — not the ones who will complete training and return to unchanged workflows.

---

## Reflection Prompt

Answer three questions:

1. Who are the 3–5 people in your org you would invest in first for AI upskilling, and why?

2. What specific training path (FORGE Academy level, course type, or program) would you put each on?

3. What will your team be demonstrably better at in 90 days if this plan succeeds?

**Your task:** Write your 90-day workforce plan in plain language — one paragraph per person, plus one closing sentence describing the team-level outcome. This is the briefing you bring to your deputy or CIO when requesting training budget.
