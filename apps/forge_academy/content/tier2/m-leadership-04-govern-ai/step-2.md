# Design Your AI Governance Structure

Good governance does not require a new organization. In most cases, it means extending existing structures — your security committee, your data governance board, your risk council — to include AI explicitly.

## The 4 Components of AI Governance

### 1. AI Use Case Registry
A living inventory of every AI system in operation or in development. Each entry should include: system name, owner, data types processed, risk classification, human oversight level, and OMB M-25-21 compliance status.

**Map to your org:** Which existing system or process would host this registry? (Options: IT asset inventory, GRC tool, SharePoint, JIRA)

### 2. Oversight Committee
A named body with authority to approve high-risk AI deployments, review incidents, and retire systems that fail performance or ethics standards.

**Map to your org:** Does your existing risk council, ISSO governance board, or CIO advisory committee have a charter that could be extended?

### 3. Policy Framework
At minimum: an AI Acceptable Use Policy, an AI Procurement Standard, and an AI Incident Response procedure.

**Map to your org:** Which policy owner (CISO, General Counsel, CAIO) would own each? Are any of these already in draft?

### 4. Incident Response
A documented process for: detecting AI failures, containing impact, notifying stakeholders, conducting root cause analysis, and updating the system or governance posture accordingly.

**Map to your org:** Does your current IT incident response playbook cover AI-specific scenarios (wrong outputs, model drift, adversarial inputs)?
