# Your AI Briefing Card

You have completed the AI Executive Primer. You now have a working understanding of what AI is, where it delivers value in government and DoD contexts, and what risks you are responsible for owning.

The final step is synthesis. An AI Briefing Card is a one-page summary — the artifact you share with a peer executive, a board member, or a Congressional staffer to demonstrate informed AI leadership.

## The AI Briefing Card Format

A strong briefing card is five short responses, each grounded in your specific organizational context — not generic statements about AI.

---

### Fill in Your Five Sections

**1. What AI can do for my organization**
Name two to three specific capabilities that apply to your mission context. Use the use cases from Step 2 as a starting point. Be specific — not "AI can help us work faster" but "AI can reduce document review time from 8 hours to 90 minutes per contract cycle."

**2. What risks I own**
Name the two to three risks from Step 3 that are most relevant to your organization's current AI posture. State whether your current mitigation is adequate or whether it is a gap.

**3. What I would invest in first**
One initiative, one sentence. Specific enough that someone could request a budget line for it.

**4. What I need from my team**
What do you need your CIO, CISO, program managers, or analysts to do differently, starting now? Name the role and the ask.

**5. What I would brief to leadership**
One paragraph — three to five sentences — that you could deliver in a 3-minute executive brief. Include: what AI is doing for the organization, what risk posture looks like, and what the next decision is.
