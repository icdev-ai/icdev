# AI Risks — The 5 You Must Own

AI capability comes with AI risk. As an executive, you do not need to understand the technical mechanics of each risk — but you must be able to name them, describe their organizational impact, and brief your mitigation posture. These are the five risks Congress asks about.

## The Five Executive-Level AI Risks

### 1. Hallucination / Wrong Answers
**Definition:** The AI produces a confident, fluent response that is factually incorrect — and the user does not know it without independent verification.
**Mitigation:** Require human review of all AI outputs used in decisions. Use RAG systems with citation grounding to reduce hallucination in high-stakes use cases.

### 2. Data Leakage
**Definition:** Sensitive, proprietary, or classified data is inadvertently processed by an AI system with insufficient access controls, potentially exposing it to unauthorized parties or vendor training pipelines.
**Mitigation:** Enforce data boundary controls before deploying any AI tool. Prohibit staff from entering CUI, PII, or classified data into commercial AI platforms without authorization.

### 3. Model Bias
**Definition:** The AI system produces outcomes that systematically disadvantage specific groups, leading to inequitable hiring, promotion, procurement, or resource allocation decisions.
**Mitigation:** Require bias testing before deployment on any AI system used in consequential decision processes. Audit outputs periodically after deployment.

### 4. Adversarial Manipulation
**Definition:** An adversary deliberately crafts inputs — documents, queries, data — designed to manipulate the AI into producing a wrong or harmful output.
**Mitigation:** Apply adversarial testing to AI systems in high-risk operational contexts. Do not deploy AI without human override capability in any system that could affect force, safety, or critical infrastructure.

### 5. Workforce Displacement
**Definition:** AI automation eliminates roles faster than the workforce can adapt, creating capability gaps, morale risk, and potential legal exposure.
**Mitigation:** Proactively communicate AI's augmentation role. Pair AI deployment with workforce upskilling investment. Never deploy automation that reduces headcount without a transition plan.

**Your task:** For each of the five risks, identify whether your organization has a current mitigation in place (yes / partial / no). Where the answer is no or partial, that is a governance gap to bring to your CISO and CAIO.
