# What Is an LLM?

A large language model is a very well-read analyst who has processed an enormous amount of text — books, articles, reports, websites — and can respond to almost any question with a fluent, confident-sounding answer. The critical difference from a human analyst: this analyst cannot verify facts in real time, cannot check whether the world has changed since training ended, and has no awareness of classified or proprietary information your organization holds.

## In Plain English

An LLM is a prediction machine. Given a question, it predicts what a useful answer would look like — based on patterns in everything it has read. When that prediction is well-grounded, the answer is remarkably good. When the prediction outpaces the available evidence, the answer can be confident and wrong simultaneously. This is called hallucination.

## What LLMs Can Do

- Summarize long documents quickly and accurately
- Draft text in any format (briefings, emails, reports, code)
- Answer questions about topics they were trained on
- Translate between formats, languages, and levels of detail

## What LLMs Cannot Do

- Access information that was not in their training data
- Verify that a specific fact is still current
- Reason with classified or proprietary data (unless integrated via RAG — covered in the Analyst Track)
- Make decisions — they produce outputs that inform decisions, not substitutes for them

## Three Types of AI Your Organization Will Encounter

- **RAG Assistants** — LLMs connected to your document library; answer questions with citations from your own data
- **Agents** — LLMs that take sequences of actions (search, summarize, draft, send) on your behalf
- **Fine-Tuned Models** — LLMs retrained on domain-specific data to improve accuracy for specialized tasks
