# AI ROI Framework

Every dollar spent on AI must be justified with the same rigor as any capital investment. The difference is that AI returns are often indirect — speed, judgment quality, analyst capacity — which makes the business case harder to build and easier to dismiss.

## Why AI Investments Need a Cost/Benefit Lens

AI is not free. Infrastructure, licensing, integration, retraining, and ongoing oversight all carry real costs. Without a structured ROI lens, AI programs get funded on hype and canceled on disappointment.

## Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) — The Three Buckets

- **Infrastructure** — Compute (cloud or on-premise), storage, networking, security controls
- **People** — Engineers to build, analysts to operate, leaders to govern, legal to review
- **Opportunity cost** — What you are not doing because your team is standing up AI instead of delivering mission

## The Three Types of Return

| Return Type | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct savings** | Reduced hours or headcount for a specific task | 3 FTEs freed from manual document review |
| **Productivity gains** | Same staff delivers more output | Analyst produces 4 reports instead of 2 per week |
| **New-capability value** | You can now do things that were previously impossible | Real-time anomaly detection across 10M records |

## Why New-Capability Value Is the Most Important — and the Hardest to Quantify

Direct savings are easy to defend in a budget briefing. New capabilities are harder — they require you to articulate what mission outcome becomes possible that wasn't before. That story is worth the effort to tell.
