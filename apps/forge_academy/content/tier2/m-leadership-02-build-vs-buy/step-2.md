# Decision Matrix: Score Your AI Initiative

A decision matrix makes trade-offs visible and auditable. It forces explicit prioritization rather than gut-feel conclusions that collapse under scrutiny.

## The Six-Criteria Matrix

Score each criterion 1–5 for your hypothetical AI assistant project. Higher is better for Buy/Partner; note where Build scores higher.

| Criterion | Buy Score (1–5) | Build Score (1–5) | Partner Score (1–5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Data Sovereignty** | Does vendor meet your boundary? | Full control | Depends on contract |
| **Customization Needed** | Low if commodity task | High if unique workflow | Medium — SI adapts |
| **Time-to-Value** | Fastest | Slowest | Middle |
| **Total Cost (3-year)** | License + integration | Engineering + maintenance | Contract + oversight |
| **Team Capability** | Minimal AI staff needed | Requires ML engineers | Requires oversight staff |
| **Vendor Lock-in Risk** | High if proprietary | None | Medium |

## Worked Example: Internal AI Assistant for Policy Review

| Criterion | Buy | Build | Partner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Data Sovereignty | 3 (FedRAMP High required) | 5 | 4 |
| Customization | 3 (generic LLM) | 5 (custom policy corpus) | 4 |
| Time-to-Value | 5 (90 days) | 1 (18+ months) | 3 (9 months) |
| Total Cost | 4 ($200K/yr) | 2 ($1.5M build) | 3 ($600K) |
| Team Capability | 5 (no ML staff needed) | 2 (need 4 FTEs) | 4 |
| Lock-in Risk | 2 | 5 | 3 |
| **Total** | **22** | **20** | **23** |

In this example, **Partner** edges out Buy on customization flexibility — but only if you have a qualified SI on contract.
