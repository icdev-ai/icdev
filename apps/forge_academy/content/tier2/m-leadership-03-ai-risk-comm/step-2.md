# Build a Risk Communication Framework

Risk communication is not a one-time event — it is a structured, repeatable process that keeps stakeholders appropriately informed without causing alarm or complacency.

## The 3-Tier Communication Structure

### Tier 1: Technical Risk → Business Impact Translation
Every technical risk has a business consequence. Your job is to complete the translation before it reaches a board member or Congress staffer.

**Template:** "If [technical failure] occurs, then [business/mission impact], affecting [who] within [timeframe]."

### Tier 2: Mitigation Posture
State what controls are in place. Be specific — vague assurances ("we take security seriously") damage credibility.

**Template:** "We mitigate this by [specific control], monitored by [role], reviewed [frequency]."

### Tier 3: Residual Risk Acceptance
After mitigation, some risk remains. Name it. State who has accepted it and at what level of authority.

**Template:** "Residual risk is [low/medium/high]. This has been accepted by [role/committee] as of [date]."
