# Your AI Incident Communications Plan

The question is not whether your AI system will produce a wrong output. It will. The question is whether you will be ready when it happens.

## Why This Matters

Without a pre-planned communications strategy, a single AI error can escalate from a technical incident into a Congressional hearing. With one, you control the narrative, maintain stakeholder trust, and demonstrate responsible AI stewardship.

## The First 24-Hour Playbook

A credible 24-hour response covers four moves:

1. **Acknowledge** — Confirm the issue is known and under investigation. Do not speculate on cause.
2. **Contain** — State what operational steps have been taken to limit impact (system paused, outputs flagged for review, human override engaged).
3. **Contextualize** — Explain the scope: how many decisions were affected, what types of outputs are in question.
4. **Commit** — State the next communication milestone and who is responsible for it.

## What Not to Say in the First 24 Hours

- "The AI made a mistake." (Use: "Outputs require human review pending investigation.")
- "We are not sure what happened." (Use: "We are actively investigating root cause.")
- "This has never happened before." (This is rarely true and rarely verifiable.)

---

## Reflection Prompt

Think about your most critical AI system or AI-influenced decision process.

Write your first 24-hour stakeholder message — 3 to 5 sentences — using the four-move structure above.

Consider: Who are your stakeholders? What channel do they use (briefing, email, formal notification)? Who is the named point of contact?

---

**Your task:** Draft your first 24-hour message now, before an incident occurs. Review it with your communications lead. A pre-positioned statement reviewed in advance is worth ten times more than one written under pressure.
