## 1. Role Taxonomy

### 1.1 Business Development (Pre-Capture)

#### VP of Business Development / BD Director
- **Level**: Senior executive, reports to CEO/COO
- **Responsibilities**:
  - Develop and execute long-term growth strategies driving revenue expansion
  - Identify new market opportunities and analyze market trends
  - Oversee multiple BD teams or divisions
  - Set annual revenue targets and pipeline value goals
  - Manage relationships with agency senior leadership
  - Approve bid/no-bid decisions for large opportunities (typically >$50M)
  - Attend industry days, pre-solicitation conferences, and agency forecasts
- **Tools**: GovWin IQ ($10K-$25K/yr), Bloomberg Government ($7.5K-$10K/yr), Federal Compass ($12K-$18K/yr), Salesforce GovCloud, Deltek Vantagepoint CRM
- **Key Metrics**: Pipeline value, win rate, pipeline-to-revenue conversion, new market penetration
- **Pain Points**: Maintaining 3:1 pipeline-to-revenue ratio, balancing pursuit costs vs. win probability, visibility into capture team progress across multiple pursuits

#### Business Development Manager / BD Analyst
- **Level**: Mid-level, reports to VP BD or BD Director
- **Responsibilities**:
  - Scan SAM.gov, agency forecasts, and market intel platforms daily
  - Maintain pipeline database with opportunity details and status
  - Conduct initial opportunity qualification (fit, past performance, competition)
  - Prepare opportunity briefs for leadership review
  - Attend industry days, record key intelligence
  - Track competitor awards and contract modifications
  - Monitor incumbent performance (CPARS, press releases, FPDS data)
- **Tools**: SAM.gov, GovWin IQ, FPDS.gov, USASpending.gov, Bloomberg Government, Federal Compass, Capture2Proposal, NextStage
- **Pain Points**: Information overload from multiple sources, manual data entry across systems, difficulty tracking pre-solicitation intelligence, no single source of truth

#### Competitive Intelligence Analyst
- **Level**: Specialized mid-level role (often combined with BD Analyst at smaller firms)
- **Responsibilities**:
  - Build and maintain competitor profiles (capabilities, key personnel, pricing patterns)
  - Analyze past award data to identify competitor bid patterns
  - Research incumbent performance on target contracts
  - Identify potential teaming partners based on capability gaps
  - Prepare competitive assessment matrices for capture reviews
  - Monitor competitor press releases, job postings, and organizational changes
- **Tools**: GovWin IQ (analyst-curated competitor data), Federal Compass (competitive positioning), FPDS (award history), SAM.gov (entity data), LinkedIn (personnel tracking), USASpending.gov
- **Pain Points**: Competitor data scattered across multiple paid platforms, difficulty correlating pricing intelligence across contracts, time-intensive manual research

### 1.2 Capture Management

#### Capture Manager / Capture Director
- **Level**: Senior individual contributor or director, single point of accountability per opportunity
- **Responsibilities**:
  - Own the opportunity from qualification through proposal submission
  - Develop and execute capture plan (customer engagement, solution shaping, competitive positioning)
  - Conduct gap analysis: identify technical or past performance deficiencies
  - Identify and recruit teaming partners (subcontractors for capabilities, agency intimacy, or socio-economic status)
  - Negotiate teaming agreements (NDAs, TAs, JVAs)
  - Lead bid/no-bid and go/no-go decision briefings
  - Build customer relationships: attend industry briefings, schedule agency meetings
  - Study past award patterns, contract deliverables, incumbent performance, pricing models
  - Develop win strategy, win themes, and discriminators
  - Prepare and present Pwin (Probability of Win) assessments
  - Manage B&P (Bid & Proposal) budget for the pursuit
  - Hand off to Proposal Manager at RFP release
- **Tools**: CaptureExec (AI-powered capture), OneTeam (cloud-native BD platform), Octant (SharePoint-based), Federal Compass (Pwin scoring), Salesforce GovCloud, Microsoft Teams, SharePoint
- **Certifications**: Shipley Capture Management, APMP (helpful but not required)
- **Pwin Scoring Factors**: Customer relationship (30%), solution fit (25%), competitive position (20%), past performance (15%), pricing competitiveness (10%) -- typical weightings vary by firm
- **Bid/No-Bid Criteria**:
  - Pgo score < 40% = no-go decision
  - Pgo 41-65% = executive review required
  - Pgo > 66% = pursue
- **Pain Points**:
  - Managing 5-15 simultaneous captures at different maturity stages
  - Difficulty quantifying customer intimacy and relationship strength
  - Teaming partner identification is largely relationship-driven, not data-driven
  - No good tools for tracking shaping activities and their effectiveness
  - Capture plan templates rarely reflect actual workflow
  - Transition from capture to proposal is often messy (knowledge loss)

### 1.3 Proposal Team (Post-RFP Release)

#### Proposal Manager / Proposal Director
- **Level**: Mid-to-senior, central coordinator for entire proposal effort
- **Responsibilities**:
  - Analyze RFP requirements (Sections L, M, C, and all attachments)
  - Develop proposal outline mapped to Section L instructions
  - Create compliance matrix mapping Section L (instructions), Section M (evaluation criteria), and Section C (SOW)
  - Prepare milestone schedule with all color team review dates
  - Assemble and coordinate cross-functional proposal team
  - Conduct kickoff meeting: provide opportunity overview, assignments, templates, schedule
  - Manage daily standup calls during proposal development
  - Track section status, identify blockers, escalate issues
  - Ensure win themes and discriminators are woven throughout all volumes
  - Coordinate color team reviews (Blue, Pink, Red, Green, Gold)
  - Manage editorial, graphics, desktop publishing, and production
  - Ensure final submission is compliant, complete, and on time
  - Manage B&P budget for proposal phase
- **Tools**: Privia/XaitPorter (co-authoring), RFPIO/Responsive (content library), VisibleThread (compliance checking, readability), Microsoft Word/SharePoint, Adobe InDesign, GovDash, Unanet ProposalAI, GovSignals
- **Certifications**: APMP Foundation/Practitioner/Professional (18-31% salary premium), Shipley (various courses), PMP
- **Career Path**: Proposal Coordinator -> Proposal Writer -> Proposal Manager -> Senior Proposal Manager -> Director of Proposals -> VP of Proposal Operations
- **Pain Points**:
  - 80% of APMP members report overwork, burnout, or emotional distress
  - Managing multiple simultaneous proposals with overlapping deadlines
  - SMEs miss internal deadlines; leadership feedback arrives late or contradicts earlier direction
  - "Just one more change" syndrome right before submission
  - High turnover means constantly training new team members
  - Content library is poorly maintained, requiring rewrites of material that should be reusable
  - Compliance matrix creation is manual and error-prone
  - No good way to track win theme implementation across volumes
  - Transition from capture to proposal loses institutional knowledge

#### Volume Lead / Technical Volume Lead / Book Boss
- **Level**: Senior SME assigned per proposal volume
- **Responsibilities**:
  - Oversee development of assigned volume (Technical, Management, Past Performance, Staffing, etc.)
  - Collaborate with SMEs to develop comprehensive technical solutions
  - Ensure volume is compliant with RFP requirements and consistent with proposal outline
  - Include win themes, discriminators, and illustrations as directed by Proposal Manager
  - Coordinate multiple writers within the volume
  - Meet scheduled deadlines for drafts and review incorporation
  - Develop innovative technical solutions that differentiate from competitors
  - Ghost the competition subtly (highlight risks of alternative approaches)
  - Ensure the technical approach addresses evaluation factors with heaviest weighting
  - Work with Solution Architect to translate solution into compelling narrative
- **Skills Required**: Deep domain expertise, strong writing ability, understanding of government evaluation processes, leadership skills, ability to translate complex concepts into evaluator-friendly language
- **Pain Points**:
  - Writers frequently have day jobs and are "loaned" to proposals part-time
  - Difficulty getting consistent level of quality across multiple authors
  - Must translate engineering/technical thinking into evaluator-friendly language
  - Often pulled into proposal at the last minute without adequate capture context
  - Section L/M analysis expertise varies widely

#### Solution Architect (Proposal Context)
- **Level**: Senior technical role
- **Responsibilities**:
  - Design the technical solution during capture phase
  - Translate customer requirements into technical architecture
  - Identify technical risks and develop mitigations
  - Create technical approach diagrams and solution architecture visuals
  - Work with Volume Lead to ensure solution is accurately represented
  - Address "as-is" vs. "to-be" state narrative
  - Demonstrate past success with similar technical challenges
  - Support Blue Team and Red Team reviews with technical validation
- **Pain Points**: Balancing optimal technical solution vs. what's affordable within price constraints, difficulty understanding evaluation criteria nuances

#### Proposal Writer
- **Level**: Individual contributor, may be staff or surge consultant
- **Responsibilities**:
  - Draft assigned sections following annotated outlines and writing plans
  - Incorporate win themes, discriminators, and proof points
  - Write to evaluation criteria emphasis
  - Incorporate review feedback from color team reviews
  - Ensure compliance with page limits, formatting requirements
  - Maintain consistency with overall proposal narrative
- **Pain Points**: Tight deadlines (often 10-30 day turnarounds for task orders), receiving incomplete storyboards or annotated outlines, contradictory feedback from multiple reviewers

#### Proposal Coordinator / Proposal Operations
- **Level**: Entry to mid-level, critical operational support
- **Responsibilities**:
  - Create, distribute, and track data calls to SMEs
  - Develop and manage proposal templates
  - Coordinate input from various SMEs and track completion
  - Manage proposal document library and version control
  - Handle logistics for color team reviews (scheduling, room booking, materials prep)
  - Manage production: printing, binding, electronic submission assembly
  - Track and distribute RFP amendments
  - Maintain past performance database
  - Handle security clearance verification for proposed personnel
- **Tools**: SharePoint, Teams, Box, proprietary document management systems
- **Career Path**: Proposal Coordinator -> Proposal Writer -> Proposal Manager
- **Pain Points**: Managing competing priorities from multiple simultaneous proposals, SMEs who don't respond to data calls, version control nightmares across multiple reviewers

#### Editor / Technical Editor
- **Level**: Mid-level specialist
- **Responsibilities**:
  - Refine proposal content for grammar, readability, clarity
  - Ensure consistent use of conventions, terminology, and style
  - Verify compliance with RFP formatting requirements (page limits, margins, fonts)
  - Check internal cross-references and figure numbering
  - Ensure acronyms are defined at first use
  - Apply "one voice" consistency across multiple authors
- **Pain Points**: Receiving near-final drafts with very little time for thorough editing, inconsistent quality from multiple writers

#### Graphics / Desktop Publishing Specialist
- **Level**: Specialist, may be staff or contractor
- **Responsibilities**:
  - Translate complex data and concepts into compelling visual infographics
  - Create process flow diagrams, org charts, schedules, and technical architecture visuals
  - Develop proposal templates compliant with RFP formatting requirements
  - Integrate text and graphics into finished layout
  - Maintain configuration management of all visual assets
  - Support "action caption" development (captions that sell, not just label)
  - Ensure visual consistency across all volumes
- **Tools**: Adobe InDesign (primary for layout), Adobe Illustrator (graphics), Microsoft Visio, PowerPoint
- **Pain Points**: Receiving vague direction on what visuals to create, last-minute changes requiring complete re-layout, page limit constraints forcing graphic removal

### 1.4 Pricing Team

#### Pricing Manager / Pricing Analyst
- **Level**: Specialized role, often sits in Finance or Contracts department
- **Responsibilities**:
  - Develop cost models for proposals (labor, ODCs, subcontractors, materials)
  - Calculate indirect rates (overhead, G&A, fringe) and wrap rates
  - Ensure DCAA compliance and CAS (Cost Accounting Standards) adherence
  - Prepare basis of estimate documentation
  - Conduct Price-to-Win (PTW) analysis
  - Support Green Team pricing review
  - Prepare cost volumes in required formats
  - Manage pricing for LPTA (Lowest Price Technically Acceptable) vs. Best Value evaluations
  - Coordinate with subcontractor pricing inputs
  - Ensure TINA (Truth in Negotiations Act) compliance for large contracts
- **Tools**: Deltek ProPricer (government proposal pricing), Deltek CostPoint (ERP/accounting), Excel (still dominant for ad-hoc models), PRICE Systems
- **Key Knowledge**: FAR Part 31 (Cost Principles), CAS Board standards, DCAA audit requirements, labor category mapping to OPM GS/SCA schedules, indirect rate calculations
- **Pain Points**:
  - Pricing information arrives late from subcontractors
  - Technical team changes solution after pricing is developed
  - Difficulty reconciling CostPoint actual rates with forward-pricing rates
  - PTW analysis requires competitive intelligence that is hard to obtain
  - Different contract types (FFP, T&M, CPFF, CPAF) require different pricing strategies
  - Small changes in indirect rates dramatically affect total evaluated price

### 1.5 Contracts & Compliance

#### Contracts Manager / Contracts Administrator
- **Level**: Mid-to-senior, legal/regulatory expertise
- **Responsibilities**:
  - Review and negotiate teaming agreements, NDAs, subcontracts
  - Ensure FAR/DFARS clause compliance in proposals
  - Advise on contract type implications (FFP, T&M, CPFF, CPAF, IDIQ)
  - Manage OCI (Organizational Conflict of Interest) screening and mitigation
  - Flow down required FAR clauses to subcontractors
  - Support contract negotiations post-award
  - Manage contract modifications and options
  - Ensure compliance with Section 889, CMMC, and DFARS cybersecurity requirements
- **Key Knowledge**: FAR Parts 1-53, DFARS, CAS, TINA, Section 889 (Huawei/ZTE ban), CMMC 2.0
- **Pain Points**: OCI determinations are complex and high-stakes (wrong call = exclusion), FAR changes frequently require updating institutional processes, managing flow-downs across multiple subcontractors

#### Suitability / Compliance Manager (Proposal Context)
- **Level**: Specialist, critical for complex proposals
- **Responsibilities**:
  - Parse RFP Sections L (Instructions) and M (Evaluation Criteria) for all requirements
  - Build and maintain compliance matrix
  - Map every RFP requirement to a specific proposal section and page
  - Verify proposal addresses all "shall," "must," and "will" statements
  - Track compliance status through all review cycles
  - Verify formatting compliance (page limits, margins, font sizes, file naming)
  - Confirm proposal cross-references are accurate
- **Pain Points**: RFP amendments change requirements mid-proposal, ambiguous requirements that require interpretation, evaluators may score differently than L/M language suggests

### 1.6 Teaming & Subcontracting

#### Subcontracts Manager (Teaming Context)
- **Level**: Mid-to-senior specialist
- **Responsibilities**:
  - Identify and vet potential teaming partners
  - Negotiate teaming agreements, joint ventures, and subcontracts
  - Manage OCI screening for team members
  - Verify socio-economic status certifications (8(a), SDVOSB, HUBZone, WOSB)
  - Coordinate subcontractor past performance and capability inputs
  - Manage subcontractor pricing integration
  - Ensure small business subcontracting plan compliance (FAR 52.219-9)
  - Track team member exclusivity and conflicts across multiple pursuits
- **Pain Points**: Teaming partners promise exclusivity then team with competitors, difficulty verifying partner capabilities until performance begins, small business goal compliance is complex and audited

### 1.7 Specialized Proposal Roles

#### Orals Coach / Presentation Coach
- **Level**: Senior specialist, often external consultant
- **Responsibilities**:
  - Analyze RFP for oral presentation requirements (Section L/M scoring criteria)
  - Develop presentation slide deck aligned with evaluation factors
  - Script presentation content mapping to Technical Evaluation Board (TEB) scoring
  - Coach individual presenters on delivery, body language, timing
  - Run mock evaluation panels with realistic Q&A
  - Prepare team for government-specific presentation constraints (no handouts, timed, recorded)
  - Develop contingency responses for likely Q&A topics
- **Key Knowledge**: FAR 15.102 (Oral Presentations), agency-specific oral procedures, TEB scoring methodologies
- **Pain Points**: Limited preparation time (often <2 weeks), presenters are technical experts not trained speakers, government oral formats vary significantly by agency

#### Proposal Operations Director / Proposal Center Manager
- **Level**: Senior management, oversees all proposal operations
- **Responsibilities**:
  - Manage proposal center staffing and resource allocation across all active pursuits
  - Maintain proposal content library and knowledge management systems
  - Develop and enforce proposal processes, templates, and standards
  - Track proposal center metrics (win rates, B&P efficiency, on-time submission rates)
  - Manage surge staffing during high-demand periods
  - Oversee proposal "war room" operations
  - Conduct post-submission White Hat reviews and lessons learned
  - Own the proposal management playbook
- **Pain Points**: Balancing resources across competing proposals, justifying B&P budget, maintaining quality with surge staff who lack institutional knowledge

---

### 1.8 Post-Award Transition

#### Program Manager (Proposal-to-Delivery Transition)
- **Level**: Senior, accountable for contract execution
- **Responsibilities**:
  - Develop transition plan (from award to full performance)
  - Execute staffing plan: recruit proposed key personnel
  - Establish contract governance structure
  - Capture incumbent knowledge during transition-in
  - Deliver Phase-In milestones per transition plan
  - Manage ongoing contract performance
  - Support re-compete proposals when contract approaches expiration
- **Transition Plan Elements**: Responsibility assignment matrix (RAM), staffing timeline, facility setup, IT infrastructure, security clearance processing, knowledge transfer from incumbent
- **Pain Points**: Proposed staff may not be available at award, transition plan in proposal may not reflect operational reality, incumbent resistance during transition

## 2. Process Map: Proposal Lifecycle

### 2.1 Shipley Business Development Lifecycle

```
MARKET ASSESSMENT -> OPPORTUNITY TRACKING -> CAPTURE PLANNING -> PROPOSAL DEVELOPMENT -> POST-SUBMISSION
     (Years out)      (12-18 months)         (6-12 months)       (30-90 days)           (30-120 days)
```

### 2.2 Detailed Lifecycle Phases

#### Phase 0: Market Assessment (12-36 months before RFP)
- **Owner**: VP BD / BD Director
- **Activities**: Industry scanning, agency budget monitoring, capability gap analysis
- **Gate**: Interest Decision — "Is this our market?"
- **Tools**: GovWin IQ, Bloomberg Government, agency Congressional Budget Justifications

#### Phase 1: Opportunity Identification (12-18 months before RFP)
- **Owner**: BD Manager / BD Analyst
- **Activities**: Pre-solicitation monitoring, RFI responses, industry day attendance
- **Gate**: Pursuit Decision — "Should we track this opportunity?"
- **Pgo Scoring**: Strategic fit, resource availability, ROI, customer relationship
- **Tools**: SAM.gov, GovWin IQ, Federal Compass, CRM/pipeline tools

#### Phase 2: Capture Planning (6-12 months before RFP)
- **Owner**: Capture Manager
- **Activities**:
  - Customer engagement (agency meetings, demos, white papers)
  - Competitive analysis (incumbent profiling, competitor capabilities)
  - Solution development (technical approach conceptualization)
  - Team building (teaming partner identification and negotiation)
  - Win strategy development (themes, discriminators, ghosts)
  - Black Hat review (think like the competitor)
  - Pwin assessment and refinement
- **Gate**: Bid/No-Bid Decision — "Should we bid?"
- **Decision Criteria**: Pwin > 30% (typical threshold), B&P budget available, key personnel identified, teaming partners committed
- **Color Team**: **Blue Team** review of capture plan and win strategy
- **Tools**: CaptureExec, OneTeam, SharePoint, capture plan templates

#### Phase 3: Proposal Development (30-90 days, or 10-15 days for task orders)
- **Owner**: Proposal Manager
- **Sub-phases**:

  **3a. RFP Analysis & Kickoff (Days 1-3)**
  - Parse Sections C, L, M for all requirements
  - Build compliance matrix
  - Develop proposal outline
  - Assemble proposal team
  - Conduct kickoff meeting
  - Distribute writing assignments and templates

  **3b. Solution Refinement & Storyboarding (Days 3-10)**
  - Refine technical solution with Volume Leads
  - Develop annotated outlines (storyboards) for each section
  - Identify proof points, past performance examples, graphics concepts
  - **Pink Team** review: Strategy alignment, compliance, responsiveness

  **3c. First Draft Development (Days 10-25)**
  - Writers produce first drafts following annotated outlines
  - Daily standups to track progress and identify blockers
  - Graphics development in parallel

  **3d. Red Team Review (Days 25-30)**
  - Simulates government evaluation process
  - Reviewers score each section against evaluation criteria
  - Identifies compliance gaps, weak themes, unclear messaging
  - Produces rated findings: Outstanding/Good/Acceptable/Marginal/Unacceptable

  **3e. Red Team Recovery (Days 30-40)**
  - Incorporate Red Team findings
  - Rewrite weak sections
  - Strengthen win themes and discriminators

  **3f. Green Team Review (Pricing) (Days 35-45)**
  - Review all pricing information for completeness and compliance
  - Verify cost model consistency with technical approach
  - Ensure company pricing policies followed
  - Check contract type-specific requirements

  **3g. Gold Team Review (Days 45-50)**
  - Final executive review
  - Confirm all Red and Green Team changes incorporated
  - Approval decision for submission
  - **Go/No-Go Gate**: Final authority to submit

  **3h. Production & Submission (Days 50-55)**
  - Final editing and desktop publishing
  - Compliance verification
  - Electronic assembly and upload (or physical printing/shipping)
  - Quality check of submitted files

#### Phase 4: Post-Submission
- **Activities**:
  - Prepare for Evaluation Notices (ENs) / Clarification Requests
  - Oral presentation preparation (if required)
  - Best and Final Offer (BAFO) preparation
  - Award notification monitoring
  - **White Hat Review**: Lessons learned, process improvement
  - Debrief request (win or lose)
  - Knowledge capture: update content library, competitor intelligence
- **Gate**: Contract award or protest decision

### 2.3 IDIQ Task Order Proposal Factory (Rapid Response)

For contractors holding IDIQ/GWAC/MATOC vehicles, a "Proposal Factory" model operates on compressed timelines:

| Turnaround | Schedule |
|-----------|----------|
| 10-day TO | Day 1: RFP analysis & kickoff. Days 2-3: Storyboard. Days 4-6: Draft. Day 7: Red Team. Days 8-9: Recovery. Day 10: Submit. |
| 15-day TO | Add 2 days for solution development and 3 days for more thorough review |
| 30-day TO | Full color team sequence (abbreviated) |

- Cuts proposal effort by 30%+ through templates, pre-approved content, and standardized processes
- Requires standing team with pre-assigned roles
- Content library must be current and well-indexed

### 4.2 By Lifecycle Phase

#### Pre-Capture Pain Points
1. **Information fragmentation**: Opportunity data spread across SAM.gov, GovWin, BGOV, FPDS, agency forecasts -- no unified view
2. **Competitor intelligence gaps**: Award pricing data hard to obtain, competitor capabilities largely anecdotal
3. **Pipeline management discipline**: Inconsistent opportunity qualification, Pwin assessment varies by individual judgment
4. **Pre-RFP customer engagement tracking**: No systematic way to log and track customer interactions and their influence

#### Capture Phase Pain Points
5. **Capture plan as shelfware**: Plans created but not actively maintained or referenced
6. **Teaming partner selection by relationships, not data**: Partner capabilities, past performance, and conflicts not systematically tracked
7. **Win strategy development is ad-hoc**: No systematic framework for translating competitive position into win themes
8. **B&P tracking**: Costs spread across multiple charge codes, hard to attribute to specific pursuits

#### Proposal Development Pain Points
9. **RFP parsing is manual**: Compliance matrix creation takes 2-5 days on large proposals
10. **Content library decay**: Past performance and boilerplate content quickly becomes stale
11. **Version control chaos**: Multiple reviewers editing simultaneously, track changes conflicts
12. **Win theme dilution**: Themes established in capture don't survive through all volumes
13. **Color team review inefficiency**: Reviewers provide conflicting feedback, review criteria not standardized
14. **Production crunch**: Last 48 hours are always chaotic regardless of earlier planning
15. **Amendment management**: Mid-proposal RFP amendments require re-analysis and potentially re-structuring

#### Task Order / IDIQ Pain Points
16. **Volume spikes**: Multiple TOs drop simultaneously, overwhelming proposal operations
17. **10-day turnarounds**: Insufficient time for meaningful color team reviews
18. **Standing team fatigue**: Same people cycling through TO after TO without breaks
19. **Template/content staleness**: Factory model requires constantly updated reusable content

#### Post-Submission Pain Points
20. **Debrief knowledge capture**: Lessons learned collected but rarely institutionalized into process changes
21. **Win/loss analysis shallow**: Debriefs focus on this proposal, not patterns across proposals
22. **Knowledge walks out the door**: When proposal professionals leave, their institutional knowledge is lost

#### Small Business Specific Pain Points

## 5. Institutional Knowledge Patterns

### 5.1 What "Tribal Knowledge" Exists

These are critical knowledge assets that typically reside only in experienced practitioners' heads:

1. **Agency Evaluation Patterns**: How specific agencies actually score proposals (vs. what Section M says). Example: "DISA always emphasizes STIG compliance even if the evaluation criteria don't mention it explicitly."

2. **Competitor Behavioral Patterns**: "Lockheed always prices aggressive on recompetes where they're the incumbent." / "SAIC tends to team with small businesses on full-and-open IT services contracts."

3. **Customer Relationship Maps**: Who the real decision-makers are, what their priorities are, which engineering groups they trust. Often maintained informally by capture managers.

4. **Pricing Intelligence**: Actual labor rates competitors have bid on similar contracts, derived from FPDS data analysis and debrief intelligence. Often maintained in personal spreadsheets.

5. **Win Theme Templates**: What themes actually resonate with specific agencies. "DoD prefers risk reduction and mission assurance themes over innovation themes."

6. **Past Performance Spin**: How to frame past performance to match new requirements. Which contracts to highlight, which to de-emphasize, which metrics to lead with.

7. **Subcontractor Reliability**: Track record of teaming partners -- which ones deliver quality past performance writeups, which ones always submit pricing late.

8. **Evaluator Psychology**: How to write to government evaluators (not executives). Understanding that evaluators use the compliance matrix as a scoring checklist. Knowing that evaluators read 10+ proposals and need clear, scannable content.

9. **Compliance Trap Avoidance**: Common mistakes that lead to non-compliance (e.g., exceeding page limits by ignoring that cover pages count, using wrong font sizes in headers, missing a SOW reference in Volume I).

10. **Production Tricks**: How to recover from crisis situations -- printer jams on submission day, FedConnect upload failures, last-minute key personnel changes.

### 5.2 Knowledge That Should Be Captured Systematically

| Knowledge Type | Current State | Ideal State |
|---------------|--------------|-------------|
| Agency evaluation behavior | In capture managers' heads | Searchable database by agency and contract type |
| Competitor bidding patterns | Scattered spreadsheets | Structured competitor profiles with pricing history |
| Win/loss pattern analysis | One-off debrief reports | Cross-proposal analytics showing patterns |
| Content library effectiveness | No tracking | Win rate by content block, reuse frequency |
| Teaming partner performance | Informal reputation | Structured scoring with delivery history |
| Pricing intelligence | Personal spreadsheets | Shared rate database with source attribution |
| Color team review patterns | Individual reviewer memory | Standardized rubrics with historical calibration |
| Compliance error patterns | Learned from mistakes | Checklist items derived from past failures |

## 7. Recommendations for ICDEV Proposal Genesis

### 7.1 Critical Capability Gaps to Address

Based on this research, ICDEV Proposal Genesis has strong foundations but is missing several capabilities that real proposal professionals consider essential:

#### Gap 1: Compliance Matrix Automation (HIGH PRIORITY)
- **Finding**: RFP parsing and compliance matrix creation takes 2-5 days manually. Tools like VisibleThread, GovDash, and Unanet ProposalAI have made this a table-stakes feature.
- **Current State**: ICDEV has `requirement_extractor.py` for shall statement mining but no automated compliance matrix builder that maps Section L instructions -> Section M evaluation criteria -> Section C SOW -> proposal outline.
- **Recommendation**: Build a `compliance_matrix_builder.py` tool that:
  - Parses RFP text to extract L/M/C requirements automatically
  - Maps requirements to proposal outline sections
  - Tracks compliance status through review cycles
  - Detects amendments and highlights changed requirements
  - Exports in standard matrix format (Word table, Excel, PDF)

#### Gap 2: Color Team Review Management (HIGH PRIORITY)
- **Finding**: Color team reviews (Blue, Pink, Red, Green, Gold, White Hat) are the backbone of the Shipley process. Every serious proposal operation runs them.
- **Current State**: ICDEV has no color team review tracking, scheduling, or structured review capture mechanism.
- **Recommendation**: Build a `color_review_manager.py` tool that:
  - Defines review types (Blue, Pink, Red, Green, Gold, White Hat)
  - Assigns reviewers with balanced perspectives (customer, technical, cost, management)
  - Provides structured review forms with rating scales (Outstanding to Unacceptable)
  - Captures findings per section with severity and priority
  - Tracks finding resolution through subsequent drafts
  - Generates review summary reports
  - Supports virtual/distributed reviews

#### Gap 3: Win Theme & Discriminator Tracking (MEDIUM-HIGH)
- **Finding**: Win themes and discriminators are established during capture but frequently lost/diluted during proposal writing. No existing ICDEV tool tracks win theme implementation across volumes.
- **Current State**: Response drafter uses capability mapping but doesn't track win themes as first-class objects.
- **Recommendation**: Extend the proposal data model to include:
  - Win theme registry with supporting proof points
  - Discriminator library linked to competitor ghosts
  - Per-section win theme implementation tracking
  - Win theme density scoring (frequency of theme reinforcement per volume)
  - "Ghost" competitor strategy templates

#### Gap 4: Pwin (Probability of Win) Scoring Engine (MEDIUM-HIGH)
- **Finding**: PWin is a structured, weighted scoring methodology used by virtually all mid-to-large contractors. Scores drive bid/no-bid decisions. ICDEV has `decide` reflex with basic scoring but it's not a comprehensive Pwin model.
- **Current State**: ICDEV decide reflex does basic scoring. Missing: configurable weighted factors, historical calibration, pipeline-wide Pwin tracking.
- **Recommendation**: Build a `pwin_scorer.py` tool that:
  - Implements configurable weighted scoring (customer fit, competitive position, past performance, pricing, solution strength, team)
  - Supports Pgo (pursue) and Pwin (win) as separate assessments
  - Provides historical calibration (compare predicted vs. actual outcomes)
  - Integrates with pipeline management for portfolio-level Pwin views
  - Supports multiple scoring methodologies (KPI-driven, expert judgment, blended)

#### Gap 5: Price-to-Win (PTW) Analysis Tool (MEDIUM)
- **Finding**: PTW is standard practice for competitive proposals. Requires analyzing incumbent pricing, competitor bid patterns, and government budget constraints.
- **Current State**: ICDEV has pricing support through CPMP but no dedicated PTW analysis tool.
- **Recommendation**: Build a `ptw_analyzer.py` that:
  - Analyzes FPDS award data for comparable contracts
  - Estimates competitor pricing ranges based on historical data
  - Supports LPTA vs. Best Value pricing strategies
  - Models indirect rate scenarios and their impact on total evaluated price
  - Integrates with Green Team review process

#### Gap 6: Content Library with Effectiveness Tracking (MEDIUM)
- **Finding**: Content libraries (past performance, technical boilerplate, management approaches) are critical infrastructure. Current tools surface content by keyword; best tools surface content by RFP requirement match.
- **Current State**: ICDEV has `knowledge_base.py` with RAG integration. Missing: win/loss tracking per content block, effectiveness scoring, staleness detection.
- **Recommendation**: Enhance knowledge base to:
  - Track which content blocks contributed to winning vs. losing proposals
  - Score content effectiveness by use frequency and win correlation
  - Detect stale content (>12 months since update, referenced regulation has changed)
  - Surface content by RFP requirement match (not just keyword search)
  - Support structured past performance data with CPARS-aligned categories

#### Gap 7: Capture-to-Proposal Handoff (MEDIUM)
- **Finding**: One of the most common knowledge losses occurs when a pursuit transitions from capture manager to proposal manager. Win strategy, customer intelligence, and competitive analysis get lost in transition.
- **Current State**: ICDEV has separate capture (scout, shape) and proposal (extract, map, draft) reflexes but no formal handoff mechanism.
- **Recommendation**: Build a structured capture-to-proposal handoff document that automatically assembles:
  - Customer relationship summary and key contacts
  - Competitive landscape analysis
  - Win themes and discriminators with supporting evidence
  - Teaming partner agreements and role assignments
  - Pricing guidance and budget constraints
  - Solution concept and technical approach highlights
  - Risk areas and mitigation strategies

#### Gap 8: Teaming Partner Management (MEDIUM)
- **Finding**: Teaming is critical to government contracting. Current partner selection is largely relationship-driven, not data-driven.
- **Current State**: ICDEV has `subcontractor_tracker.py` for post-award tracking but minimal capture-phase teaming support.
- **Recommendation**: Build a `teaming_manager.py` that:
  - Maintains partner capability profiles with past performance evidence
  - Tracks OCI risks per partner per opportunity
  - Scores partner reliability (on-time delivery of proposal inputs, pricing accuracy)
  - Supports socio-economic status verification (8a, SDVOSB, HUBZone, WOSB)
  - Manages exclusivity agreements and conflict tracking across pursuits
  - Identifies teaming gaps based on RFP requirements vs. team capabilities

#### Gap 9: IDIQ Task Order Proposal Factory (MEDIUM)
- **Finding**: Large contractors handle dozens of task order proposals per month with 10-15 day turnarounds. This requires a factory model with compressed processes.
- **Current State**: ICDEV pipeline handles individual proposals but doesn't support the high-volume, rapid-turnaround factory model.
- **Recommendation**: Build a `to_factory.py` module that:
  - Supports 10/15/30-day compressed proposal schedules
  - Pre-assigns standing team roles per IDIQ vehicle
  - Maintains vehicle-specific content libraries and templates
  - Automates compliance matrix creation from TO task descriptions
  - Tracks volume and capacity across multiple active TOs
  - Supports rapid pricing through pre-approved rate cards

#### Gap 10: Post-Award Intelligence Loop (LOW-MEDIUM)
- **Finding**: Win/loss debriefs contain valuable intelligence but are rarely analyzed across proposals to identify patterns.
- **Current State**: ICDEV `analyze` reflex does basic win/loss analysis.
- **Recommendation**: Enhance with:
  - Structured debrief capture templates aligned to evaluation criteria
  - Cross-proposal pattern analysis (what themes win at which agencies, common failure modes)
  - Evaluator scoring pattern analysis (how closely do agency scores track Section M weights)
  - Content library feedback loop (tag content blocks used in winning vs. losing proposals)
