# Don't put all your eggs in one stock

The single most common mistake new investors make is **concentration**:
believing in one stock so strongly they put 30%, 50%, 80% of their portfolio
into it.

This lesson is about why that hurts — and the simple rules that fix it.

## What concentration actually does to you

Say you have $10,000 and buy $8,000 of NVDA + $2,000 of "everything else."
Your NVDA position is 80% of your book.

On a 5% NVDA down-day (very common), you lose $400. On a 20% NVDA down-day
(happens 1-2 times/year during corrections), you lose $1,600 — 16% of your
portfolio — from one stock.

Even if NVDA eventually recovers, the journey is miserable: sleep, stress,
panic-selling at the bottom. Most people can't hold concentrated positions
through a real drawdown. They sell at the worst possible time.

## The fix: position sizing rules

Professional portfolio managers use rules like:

- **No single name > 5% of book** — target. Never let a winner grow past
  10-15% without trimming.
- **No single sector > 25% of book** — avoid over-loading on Tech,
  Financials, etc.
- **Minimum 10-20 names** — enough that one blowup hurts but doesn't
  destroy you.

For a beginner, start simpler: **no position bigger than 10% of your book.**
If NVDA rips +50%, trim it back to 10% and redeploy into something else.

## What diversification buys you

With 10 stocks at 10% each, one -50% crash costs you 5% of your portfolio.
Annoying, but survivable — and recoverable in a year or two of normal growth.

With 2 stocks at 50% each, one -50% crash costs you 25%. Devastating. Most
amateur investors never recover emotionally from that, even if the numbers
do recover.

## Sector spread is just as important as stock spread

Owning SPY + QQQ + VTI isn't really diversification — they all own the same
S&P 500 megacaps. If Tech crashes, all three crash.

Real sector diversification looks like:
- Tech (AAPL, MSFT, GOOGL, or QQQ)
- Healthcare (UNH, JNJ, or XLV)
- Financials (JPM, BRK-B, or XLF)
- Consumer Staples (PG, KO, or XLP)
- Energy (XOM, or XLE)
- Utilities, Industrials, etc.

The pairs that move DIFFERENTLY from each other are your real diversification.
When Tech tanks, Staples + Utilities often hold up. That's the math of it.

## The honest hard truth

You'll be tempted to break these rules when a stock you love is ripping.
**That's precisely when you should trim.** Your future self — after the
inevitable correction — will thank you.
