# Pulse Article Style Profile

Adapted from AWS Public Sector Blog writing style (compliance/GovTech content).

## Voice & Tone
- **Professional-authoritative** but accessible — not academic, not casual
- **Second person** ("you", "your") to directly address the reader
- Confident without being salesy — state facts, show paths, don't oversell
- Empathetic to pain points — acknowledge the struggle before offering solutions
- "If you've pursued FedRAMP authorization before, you know the challenges"

## Structure Pattern
1. **Opening hook** — one paragraph stating what the reader will learn and why it matters
2. **Problem acknowledgment** — briefly validate the pain (timelines, costs, manual effort)
3. **Context/Background** — explain the landscape change (new regulation, new approach)
4. **Section-by-section breakdown** — H3 headers, each covering one aspect
5. **Tables for mapping** — Service → Role, Category → Tool (readers scan tables)
6. **Phased approach** — numbered steps grouped into phases (Phase 1, Phase 2, Phase 3)
7. **Conclusion** — restate the shift, connect back to reader's advantage
8. **Next steps** — bulleted resource links

## Sentence Style
- Mix short punchy sentences with longer explanatory ones
- Lead paragraphs with the key point, then expand
- Use em dashes for asides — "not through documents, but through data"
- Colons to introduce lists within sentences: "three key obstacles: documentation, assessment, monitoring"
- Avoid passive voice — "you create", "you implement", not "is created", "is implemented"
- No filler words (basically, essentially, in order to, it is important to note)

## Paragraph Style
- 2-4 sentences per paragraph (never walls of text)
- One idea per paragraph
- First sentence = topic sentence (scannable)
- Last paragraph before a section often sets up what's next

## Technical Depth
- Name specific tools, frameworks, standards (FedRAMP, NIST, OSCAL, KSI)
- Define acronyms on first use: "Key Security Indicators (KSIs)"
- Include architecture diagrams and mapping tables
- Reference real timelines and metrics: "12-18 months", "560 hours", "20 times faster"
- Don't explain basics — assume reader knows cloud, compliance, government

## Human Voice (CRITICAL — defeats AI detection)
- **Vary sentence length dramatically** — follow a 25-word sentence with a 4-word one. Then a 15-word one. Break rhythm constantly.
- **Use sentence fragments** on purpose. For emphasis. Like this.
- **Start sentences with "And" or "But"** — LLMs avoid this. Humans don't.
- **Use contractions** — "you'll", "don't", "isn't", "we've" (never "you will", "do not")
- **Include a rhetorical question** every 2-3 paragraphs — "Sound familiar?" or "So what changed?"
- **Use unexpected word choices** — "brutal" instead of "challenging", "crushed" instead of "impacted", "slog" instead of "process"
- **Reference a specific, concrete detail** — a real tool name, a real regulation number, a real timeline from a real project
- **One imperfect transition per article** — skip the smooth connector and just jump. Readers won't notice. Detectors will.
- **Interrupt yourself** mid-thought with an em dash — then continue
- **Avoid these AI-tell phrases**: "In today's landscape", "It's worth noting", "This approach enables", "plays a crucial role", "a key consideration", "importantly", "fundamentally"

## What NOT to Do
- No "In today's rapidly evolving landscape..." openers
- No generic motivational conclusions ("The future is bright...")
- No documentation links or community resources (per user feedback)
- No GitHub links (per user feedback)
- No excessive hedging ("might", "could potentially", "it's possible that")
- No first-person ("I", "we") — always second person ("you") or third person
- No marketing speak ("revolutionary", "game-changing", "cutting-edge")

## Formatting
- H1: Article title (clear, specific, keyword-rich)
- H3: Section headers (action-oriented: "Building your trust center", "Automating vulnerability detection")
- Bold for key terms on first mention
- Tables for tool/service mappings (2-column: Service | Role)
- Bulleted lists for steps, requirements, resources
- Numbered lists for sequential phases

## Word Count Target
- 1200-1800 words (substantial but scannable)
- Each section: 150-250 words
